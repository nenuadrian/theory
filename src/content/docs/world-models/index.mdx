A model-free agent learns a single thing: a mapping from situations to actions.
It improves by trial and error in the world itself, discarding each experience
once the gradient has been taken. This is robust, but profligate — millions of
interactions are spent to learn what a moment of foresight would supply.

A **world model** changes the proposition. The agent learns a *simulator* of its
environment: a compressed account of what it perceives, together with a
prediction of how that perception will evolve as it acts. Equipped with such a
model, the agent need not consult reality for every decision. It can **plan** —
search over imagined futures and commit only to the best — and it can **learn in
imagination**, improving its policy on synthetic experience generated by the
model rather than by the world. The world becomes a quantity to be predicted, not
merely reacted to.

## Perception, memory, control

The foundational architecture — introduced by Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) and
recurring, in elaborated form, in nearly everything since — factors the agent
into three components:

- **Vision $(V)$.** A high-dimensional observation $o_t$ — a frame of pixels — is
  compressed by a [variational autoencoder](/world-models/perception/) into a
  small **latent** vector $z_t$ that retains what matters and discards the rest.
- **Memory $(M)$.** A recurrent [dynamics model](/world-models/dynamics/) carries
  a summary $h_t$ of the past and predicts the *next* latent,
  $p(z_{t+1} \mid z_t, a_t, h_t)$ — the world model proper.
- **Control $(C)$.** A small policy reads the model's state $(z_t, h_t)$ and emits
  an action. Because $V$ and $M$ have already done the representational work, $C$
  can be almost trivially small.

The decisive observation is that once $V$ and $M$ exist, $C$ may be trained
*entirely within the model* — inside a "dream" — and the resulting behaviour can
transfer back to the real environment.

## Two uses of a model

A learned model earns its cost in two distinct ways, and the modern algorithms
are organized by which one they exploit.

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Planning" icon="magnifier">
		Hold the model fixed and **search** at decision time: roll many candidate
		action sequences forward through it, score each by predicted return, and
		execute the first action of the best. PlaNet and MuZero plan.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Imagination" icon="rocket">
		Use the model as an inexhaustible **source of experience**: train an
		actor and critic on trajectories the model dreams, never touching the
		environment except to refit the model. Dreamer learns in imagination.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## What this track builds

By its end the reader will have implemented, in NumPy, the pieces that recur
across the literature: a variational autoencoder and its ELBO, a mixture-density
network that samples genuinely multimodal futures, a cross-entropy-method planner
that steers a learned model toward a goal, the $\lambda$-return that targets a
critic in imagination, and a Monte-Carlo tree search guided by a learned value.
The aim is not breadth for its own sake but a single thread: **a model of the
world, used to act.**
