The **Kullback–Leibler divergence** measures the expected number of extra bits
needed to encode samples from $p$ using a code built for $q$:

$$
\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q) = \sum_x p(x) \ln \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
\quad\Big(\text{or } \int p(x)\ln\tfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}\,dx\Big).
$$

It appeared on the [previous page](/divergences/bregman/) as the Bregman
divergence of negative entropy; it is *also* the f-divergence with
$f(t) = t\ln t$. In either case the expectation is taken under the **first**
argument, and this single fact accounts for why running KL forwards and backwards
yields such different results.

## Two directions, one model

Consider a complicated true distribution $p$ that is to be approximated by a
simpler $q$ — for instance, a single Gaussian. The KL may be minimized in either
order, and the two choices have distinct names and characteristic behaviors:

- **Forward KL** $\;\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q)\;$ — the *inclusive* direction.
  The expectation is under $p$, so wherever $p(x)$ is large the ratio $p/q$ must
  not grow without bound: $q$ is penalized severely for being near zero where $p$
  has mass. It cannot afford to ignore any part of $p$, and so it **spreads to
  cover every mode**. This is the direction maximum likelihood minimizes.
- **Reverse KL** $\;\mathrm{KL}(q \,\|\, p)\;$ — the *exclusive* direction.
  Here the expectation is under $q$, so $q$ is penalized for placing mass where
  $p \approx 0$. The favorable strategy is to **concentrate on one mode** and
  avoid the low-density valley between them. This is the direction variational
  inference and the ELBO minimize.

In summary, **forward KL is mode-covering** (zero-avoiding, mean-seeking),
whereas **reverse KL is mode-seeking** (zero-forcing).

## Observing the choice

The gray shape below is a fixed, deliberately **bimodal** target $p$. The teal
curve is a single Gaussian $q$ governed by its mean $\mu$ and width $\sigma$. The
controls vary these parameters, and both KL values respond accordingly. Each
direction may then be allowed to select its own best fit:

<KLDivergence client:visible />

- **fit forward** fixes $q$ as a *wide* bell straddling both humps. It places mass
  in the empty valley, where $p$ has almost none, rather than abandon either mode.
  The fit is overdispersed, but it covers the entire support.
- **fit reverse** fixes $q$ as a *narrow* bell centered on one mode, disregarding
  the other. The fit is sharp and confident, but it has discarded half the
  distribution.

Neither fit is incorrect; the two optimize different objectives. Forward KL
averages over all of $p$'s support, whereas reverse KL declines to assign
probability where $p$ provides no support.

<Aside type="note" title="Forward KL has an exact optimum">
	The forward fit is not obtained by grid search; it is computed in closed form.
	Minimizing $\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q)$ over a Gaussian $q$ **matches the
	moments**: $q$'s mean and variance are set equal to those of $p$. More
	generally, minimizing forward KL over any exponential family matches expected
	sufficient statistics, which is precisely what maximum likelihood does. Reverse
	KL admits no such closed form, so its fit must be found by search.
</Aside>

## Which direction to use

The choice depends entirely on the cost assigned to a wrong answer:

| | forward $\mathrm{KL}(p \,\Vert\, q)$ | reverse $\mathrm{KL}(q \,\Vert\, p)$ |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| nickname | inclusive, zero-avoiding | exclusive, zero-forcing |
| behavior | covers all modes (overdispersed) | picks one mode (can collapse) |
| needs | samples / density of $p$ | ability to evaluate $p$ up to a constant |
| shows up in | maximum likelihood, cross-entropy, distillation | variational inference, the ELBO, many RL objectives |

A practical guideline: if the foremost concern is failing to capture part of the
truth, use the **forward** direction and cover the entire support. If the foremost
concern is placing confident mass on something false, use the **reverse**
direction and commit only to what is well supported.

## Verification in code

The following reproduces both fits from first principles: it defines the bimodal
$p$, integrates each KL on a grid, obtains the forward optimum by moment matching
and the reverse optimum by search, and plots the two side by side:

	title="forward_reverse.py"
	code={`import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Target p: a bimodal mixture  (weight, mean, std)
comps = [(0.6, -2.5, 0.6), (0.4, 2.8, 0.9)]
def normal(x, m, s): return np.exp(-0.5 * ((x - m) / s) ** 2) / (s * np.sqrt(2 * np.pi))
def p_pdf(x):        return sum(w * normal(x, m, s) for w, m, s in comps)

x  = np.linspace(-10, 10, 1000); dx = x[1] - x[0]
P  = p_pdf(x)

def kl(a, b):                       # numeric KL(a || b) on the grid
    a, b = np.maximum(a, 1e-12), np.maximum(b, 1e-12)
    return np.sum(a * np.log(a / b)) * dx

# Forward optimum: match the mean and variance of p (exact).
mean = sum(w * m for w, m, _ in comps)
var  = sum(w * (s * s + m * m) for w, m, s in comps) - mean ** 2
fwd  = (mean, np.sqrt(var))

# Reverse optimum: grid-search KL(q || p) — no closed form.
best, best_kl = None, np.inf
for mu in np.arange(-5, 5.01, 0.1):
    for sig in np.arange(0.35, 3.01, 0.05):
        v = kl(normal(x, mu, sig), P)
        if v < best_kl: best_kl, best = v, (mu, sig)
rev = best

qf, qr = normal(x, *fwd), normal(x, *rev)
print(f"forward fit  N(mu={fwd[0]:+.2f}, sd={fwd[1]:.2f})   covers both modes")
print(f"reverse fit  N(mu={rev[0]:+.2f}, sd={rev[1]:.2f})   locks onto one mode")
print(f"  KL(p||q) at forward fit = {kl(P, qf):.3f}")
print(f"  KL(q||p) at reverse fit = {kl(qr, P):.3f}")

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(8.4, 3.3), sharey=True)
for ax, q, ttl in [(axes[0], qf, "forward  ·  min KL(p || q)"),
                   (axes[1], qr, "reverse  ·  min KL(q || p)")]:
    ax.fill_between(x, P, color="0.82", label="p (target)")
    ax.plot(x, q, color="#13a89a", lw=2, label="q (fit)")
    ax.set_title(ttl); ax.set_xlim(-7, 7); ax.set_xticks([]); ax.set_yticks([]); ax.legend()
fig.tight_layout()
`}
/>

<Aside type="tip" title="Try it">
	Making the split more lopsided — changing the weights to `0.8` and `0.2` — and
	re-running produces a clear contrast. The reverse fit moves decisively onto the
	heavier mode, while the forward fit only shifts its wide bell toward it. This
	demonstrates mode-seeking versus mode-covering directly.
</Aside>
