## The shared toolkit: Divergences

Both tracks rest on a common construct: a **divergence** that quantifies the
discrepancy between two distributions. It is the loss underlying maximum
likelihood, the regularizer underlying trust regions, and the reason forward and
reverse KL draw a model in opposite directions.

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Bregman divergences" icon="puzzle">
		[One convex function generating an entire family of divergences.](/divergences/bregman/)
		The divergence is the gap above a tangent line; adjust two points to observe it.
	</Card>
	<Card title="KL: forward vs reverse" icon="puzzle">
		[Mode-covering versus mode-seeking behavior.](/divergences/kl-forward-reverse/)
		Fit a single Gaussian to a bimodal target and observe how each direction selects differently.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## Then: Reinforcement Learning

The second track surveys modern policy optimization in full: the
**policy-gradient** path from REINFORCE through PPO to **GRPO**, and the
**expectation-maximization** path through MPO, V-MPO, and AWR. It establishes that
the two address the same problem from opposite directions.

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Policy Gradients → GRPO" icon="rocket">

	<Card title="The EM road: MPO → AWR" icon="puzzle">

</CardGrid>

## Finally: World Models

A reinforcement learner acts in the world. A **world model** learns a simulator of
it — perception, dynamics, and reward — and then plans or learns *inside* the model,
trading costly real interaction for cheap imagination. The track runs from the
original VAE + MDN-RNN to PlaNet, Dreamer, MuZero, and JEPA.
