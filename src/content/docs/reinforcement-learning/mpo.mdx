**Maximum a posteriori Policy Optimisation** (MPO, Abdolmaleki et al. 2018) is the
[EM loop](/reinforcement-learning/rl-as-inference/) realized as a working,
off-policy algorithm. It became a standard method for continuous control — robotics
and locomotion — precisely because the EM structure renders its updates unusually
stable. It maintains a learned critic and a replay buffer, and it resolves the one
question the inference view left open: *what temperature $\alpha$ should be used?*

## E-step: reweight, with the temperature chosen for you

Recall that the closed-form improved policy is $q(a \mid s) \propto \pi_{\text{old}}(a
\mid s)\exp(Q(s, a)/\eta)$. A fixed temperature $\eta$ is problematic: when it is too small,
$q$ collapses onto one action; when it is too large, $q$ barely improves; and the appropriate
value drifts as $Q$ changes scale during training. MPO addresses this by **not selecting
$\eta$ directly** — instead, it requires that the improved policy move a *controlled distance*
from the old one, and lets that requirement determine the temperature.

Formally, the E-step solves a KL-constrained improvement:

$$
\max_{q}\; \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{D}}\,\mathbb{E}_{a \sim q}\big[\, Q(s, a) \,\big]
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad
\mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{D}}\big[\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, \pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot \mid s)\big) \,\big] \le \epsilon_E.
$$

Its solution is the same Boltzmann form, and the Lagrange multiplier on the
constraint **is** the temperature $\eta$. One recovers $\eta$ by minimizing a
convex one-dimensional **dual**:

$$
\eta^{*} = \arg\min_{\eta > 0}\; \eta\, \epsilon_E + \eta\, \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{D}}\!\left[\, \log \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_{\text{old}}}\!\Big[\exp\!\Big(\frac{Q(s, a)}{\eta}\Big)\Big] \,\right].
$$

A brief run of gradient descent on this scalar yields the temperature that makes
the E-step move exactly $\epsilon_E$ in KL — automatically, at every iteration,
regardless of how $Q$ is scaled. In practice $q$ need not be materialized at all:
one samples $M$ actions per state from $\pi_{\text{old}}$, evaluates the critic, and
forms **self-normalized weights**

$$
w_{ij} = \frac{\exp\!\big(Q(s_i, a_{ij})/\eta\big)}{\sum_{k} \exp\!\big(Q(s_i, a_{ik})/\eta\big)}.
$$

<Aside type="note" title="The temperature dual is the whole trick">
	Hand-tuning a temperature across tasks and training stages is burdensome. MPO
	replaces it with an interpretable control — the policy is permitted to improve by $\epsilon_E$
	nats per step — and solves a small convex problem to enforce it. This is what
	makes MPO robust where a fixed-temperature method proves sensitive to its settings.
</Aside>

## M-step: weighted regression inside a trust region

With the weights in hand, the parametric policy is fit by **weighted maximum
likelihood** — the M-step from the inference page — but with an explicit
trust-region KL added so that the network update cannot overshoot the target either:

$$
\max_{\theta}\; \sum_{i, j} w_{ij}\, \log \pi_\theta(a_{ij} \mid s_i)
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad
\mathbb{E}_{s}\big[\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s)\big) \,\big] \le \epsilon_M.
$$

MPO is thus bounded **twice**: once in the E-step (limiting how far the *target* $q$ may move
from $\pi_{\text{old}}$) and once in the M-step (limiting how far the *network* may move
toward $q$). For Gaussian policies, the second constraint is **decoupled** into
separate bounds on the mean and the covariance, allowing the policy to recenter
quickly while shrinking its exploration noise slowly — a distinction that is
material for stable continuous control. Each constraint is enforced with its own Lagrange
multiplier, optimized jointly with $\theta$.

## Why it's off-policy and where it shines

The expectations above are taken over a state distribution $\mathcal{D}$, and nothing
requires $\mathcal{D}$ to originate from the current policy. MPO draws states from a
**replay buffer** and uses a critic $Q_\phi$ learned off-policy (typically via
Retrace). This makes it considerably more **sample-efficient** than on-policy
policy-gradient methods, since every transition can be reused many times. Combined with
the auto-tuned temperature and the decoupled trust region, MPO attains strong and
stable results across continuous-control benchmarks.

<Aside type="tip" title="MPO as a recipe">
	The critic $Q_\phi$ is learned off-policy. Each step proceeds as follows: (1) sample actions and score them with
	$Q_\phi$; (2) solve the scalar dual for $\eta$; (3) form weights $\propto
	\exp(Q/\eta)$; (4) perform weighted-MLE on $\pi_\theta$ under a decoupled KL trust
	region. This is the entire algorithm — the EM loop with the step-size of every step
	governed by a KL bound.
</Aside>

## What changes next

MPO's machinery — learned $Q$, off-policy replay, a temperature dual, a decoupled
trust region — is substantial. The next two pages remove components of it.
[V-MPO](/reinforcement-learning/vmpo/) replaces the learned $Q$ with on-policy
**advantages** and adds a filtering step, targeting large-scale on-policy
problems. [AWR](/reinforcement-learning/awr/) removes nearly *everything*, including the
dual, leaving the smallest version of the loop that remains effective.
