title: V-MPO
description: On-policy MPO at scale — using state-value advantages instead of a learned Q, a top-half filtering trick, and the same temperature dual and trust-region M-step.
---

[MPO](/reinforcement-learning/mpo/) is off-policy and relies on a learned
action-value $Q_\phi$. This becomes a liability at scale: when thousands
of parallel actors feed a single learner — the regime in which on-policy methods
such as PPO and IMPALA dominate — fitting an accurate $Q$ is difficult, and it is preferable to
use the fresh on-policy data directly. **V-MPO** (Song et al. 2019) adapts the EM
loop to precisely this setting, and was used to train large-scale agents across
Atari, DMLab-30, and continuous control, matching or surpassing the on-policy
baselines of its day.

Two changes render MPO on-policy.

## Change 1: advantages from a state-value critic

V-MPO discards the action-value $Q_\phi$ and retains only a **state-value** critic
$V_\phi(s)$, trained by regressing toward $n$-step returns. The learning signal
becomes the **advantage** computed from on-policy rollouts:

$$
A(s_t, a_t) = G_t^{(n)} - V_\phi(s_t),
$$

with $G_t^{(n)}$ an $n$-step (or GAE-style) return. Wherever MPO wrote $Q$,
V-MPO writes $A$. This substitution is valid because the E-step depends only on
*relative* value, and $Q$ and $A$ differ by a state-only constant that the
normalization eliminates.

Because the data is on-policy, there is typically **one action per state**.
Consequently the nonparametric target $\psi$ is no longer a per-state distribution over
actions; it is a single distribution over **all the $(s, a)$ pairs in the batch**,
weighting each by its exponentiated advantage.

## Change 2: keep only the better half

This is V-MPO's signature device. Instead of weighting every sample, it **discards
the bottom half of the batch by advantage** and constructs the target only from the
top half $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$:

$$
\psi(s, a) = \frac{\exp\!\big(A(s, a)/\eta\big)}{\sum_{(s', a') \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}} \exp\!\big(A(s', a')/\eta\big)},
\qquad (s, a) \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}.
$$

The rationale is that below-average actions carry little useful signal once the advantages have been
exponentiated, and discarding them outright reduces variance and prevents the policy from
expending capacity on imitating mediocre behavior. Empirically, this filtering
accounts for much of why V-MPO trains stably at scale.

The temperature $\eta$ is determined by the **same convex dual** as in MPO, now over the
top-half advantages:

$$
\eta^{*} = \arg\min_{\eta > 0}\; \eta\, \epsilon_\eta + \eta\, \log \frac{1}{|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|} \sum_{(s, a) \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}} \exp\!\Big(\frac{A(s, a)}{\eta}\Big).
$$

The principle is the same as before: the temperature is set so that the target moves a fixed KL
$\epsilon_\eta$ from the current policy, automatically and at any reward scale.

## The M-step is unchanged

Given the target weights, the network is fit by weighted maximum likelihood under a
trust-region KL — identical in form to MPO:

$$
\max_{\theta}\; \sum_{(s, a) \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}} \psi(s, a)\, \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad
\mathbb{E}_{s}\big[\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s)\big) \,\big] \le \epsilon_M.
$$

Both the temperature constraint $\epsilon_\eta$ and the trust-region $\epsilon_M$
are handled by Lagrange multipliers learned by gradient descent alongside
$\theta$, so the practitioner specifies two interpretable KL budgets and the algorithm
determines the remainder.

<Aside type="note" title="MPO vs V-MPO, side by side">
	**Critic:** MPO learns $Q$ off-policy, whereas V-MPO learns $V$ on-policy. **Data:** MPO
	replays a buffer, whereas V-MPO consumes fresh rollouts. **E-step weights:** MPO
	exponentiates $Q$ over actions sampled per state, whereas V-MPO exponentiates $A$ over
	the top half of a batch. **M-step and dual:** these are identical. V-MPO is MPO adapted to the
	on-policy, large-scale regime.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="The recurring shape">
	Observe that V-MPO and [PPO](/reinforcement-learning/trust-regions/) now occupy
	the *same* niche — on-policy, advantage-based, KL-controlled — yet derive their
	updates by entirely different means. PPO clips a policy-gradient ratio, whereas V-MPO
	regresses onto an exponentiated-advantage target. The two roads have converged
	on the same problem from opposite directions.
</Aside>

The final page reduces the loop to its essentials — no dual, no learned $Q$, no
trust-region solve — and shows that even this minimal form remains effective, while tying the whole
family back to the starting point: [AWR](/reinforcement-learning/awr/).
